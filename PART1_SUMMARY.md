# What 50 Major Companies Said They Valued, 2016–2024

**In one line:** we reconstructed the public "About / values" page of 50 of the largest U.S.
companies for every year from 2016 to 2024, recovered the text even where the live pages are
long gone, and measured which values each company foregrounded — and how that shifted over
nine years. This covers what companies *say*; comparing it to what they *do* is the next phase.

We targeted **50 companies** and recovered usable values text for **45**; the remaining **5**
(Apple, Ford, Intel, Oracle, and American Express) yielded no archived values page despite
documented attempts.

The findings below rest on **326 verified company-year pages across 45 companies and 13 value
themes**. (The other 5 companies and some individual years couldn't be recovered from the
archive — see *Coverage* at the end.)

---

## 1. Four values dominate corporate self-presentation

Across all companies and years, the most-stated values are **innovation** (in 63% of pages),
**customer/client focus** (61%), **community & social responsibility** (52%), and
**operational excellence** (50%). Companies typically foreground about **five** values at
once — these pages are broad mission statements, not single-value pledges.

## 2. Each sector has a distinct "values fingerprint"

What a company claims to value is strongly predictable from its industry:

| Value emphasized | Where it's high | Where it's low / absent |
|---|---|---|
| **Innovation & science** | Technology (100%), Healthcare (88%) | Financials (23%) |
| **Operational excellence** | Energy (84%), Consumer (66%) | Technology (16%) |
| **Profit & shareholder value** | Financials (69%), Energy (57%) | Tech (2%), Healthcare (2%) |
| **Safety** | Energy (46%), Healthcare (25%) | Financials (0%) |
| **Human progress / well-being** | Healthcare (98%), Tech (68%) | Financials (5%) |
| **Environmental stewardship** | Energy (46%) | fairly even elsewhere (~30%) |

The sharpest contrast: **financial firms and energy firms openly foreground profit and
shareholder value (~60–70% of pages), while tech and healthcare almost never do (2%)** —
those sectors instead frame themselves around innovation and human well-being. Safety is a
near-perfect industry marker: central for energy and healthcare, entirely absent from banks.

## 3. A clear shift from "shareholder" to "stakeholder" language over the decade

Comparing the early window (2016–2018) to the recent one (2022–2024), the *same* companies
moved their stated emphasis in one consistent direction:

- **Down: profit / shareholder value — 48% → 26%** (the single biggest move).
- **Up: environmental stewardship 27% → 45%, safety 13% → 31%, people & talent 38% → 53%,
  integrity 30% → 44%, community 49% → 62%, diversity & inclusion 20% → 30%.**

In plain terms: over 2016–2024, large companies **publicly de-emphasized making money for
shareholders and leaned into environment, people, safety, ethics, community and inclusion** —
the "ESG / stakeholder" vocabulary. This is a shift in *messaging*; whether behavior moved
with it is exactly the question Parts 2–4 are built to answer.

## 4. Most companies actively rewrote their values; a few never touched them

We detected **166 year-over-year rewrites across 39 of 45 companies** — values pages are
living documents, revised most years. Only **5 companies kept an essentially unchanged page**
for the whole period (Broadcom, Berkshire Hathaway, Bank of America, Charles Schwab,
Occidental) — Berkshire's famously plain site being the clearest example.

---

## What this is good for

- A **baseline of stated values** for 45 major firms, by year — usable on its own to track how
  a company's public positioning evolved, or to compare peers within a sector.
- The **raw material for an authenticity measure**: the natural next step is to set these
  stated values against what the same companies actually disclosed and did (regulatory
  filings, ESG reports), and flag the gaps — companies whose words moved but whose actions
  didn't.

## Coverage and honesty about gaps

The dataset is **72% complete** (326 of a possible 450 company-years). Gaps are not hidden:
each is labeled with a reason — the archive never captured that year, the page existed but
had no readable text, or the site never responded (four heavy sites: Apple, Ford, Intel,
Oracle). Five companies could not be recovered at all. Every figure above is computed only
from pages we actually recovered and read; nothing is inferred to fill a gap.
